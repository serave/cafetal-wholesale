Cafetal Coffee & Familia is a 4th-generation Colombian coffee roastery born from a family farm, a lifelong dream, and a deep belief that great coffee should tell a true story.
Sebastián Ramirez grew up in Medellín — but his roots are on the farm. His first job was picking coffee cherries alongside his familia at Finca La Piedad in Santa Bárbara, Antioquia, a farm his great-grandfather started over 60 years ago.
He studied computer science, moved to the U.S., met his wife Nikki in Colorado, then worked at Amazon in Seattle. But even while building a career in tech, the dream of something more personal never left him.
The Story of Sebastián & Nikki
When the opportunity to open a coffee shop in Tempe arrived, it felt like more than a business decision — it was a homecoming. As the son of a coffee farmer, Sebastián had seen firsthand how global coffee prices can upend families at origin. Cafetal became a way to build something more direct, more honest, more human.
Learning to roast gave him a new level of connection — not just to the product, but to his familia. Every roast cycle is a conversation between Tempe and Antioquia. The feedback goes both ways: better quality for the farm, better coffee for your cup.
"Every roast feels personal. It's our coffee, our story — from our farm in Colombia to our community here in Arizona."

Why Colombia — Only Colombia

Two harvests per year — main season Oct–Feb and the Mitaca Apr–Jun — means genuinely fresh-crop coffee year-round. We are the only roastery in Phoenix dedicated exclusively to Colombian specialty coffee.

Why Farm-Direct Matters

We own Finca La Piedad. We know every tree, every picker, every fermentation tank. That traceability is a story your baristas can tell — and your customers will remember.

About Finca La Piedad
Our family's farm sits in a beautiful valley in the mountains of Santa Bárbara, Antioquia, at 1,500–1,900 masl. Over 60 years the land was passed down among familia — three parcels still owned and operated within the family. Rich soil, dense vegetation, and high altitude create ideal conditions. We follow every Rainforest Alliance practice to protect the land, preserve biodiversity, and care for the people who make the harvest possible.

Finca La Primavera & José Oliver Galíndez

A second-generation farm in El Huila. Began as cattle pastures; by 1995 the Galíndez family planted their first coffee trees. By 2005 they introduced Pink Bourbon — one of Colombia's most prized varietals. Producer José Oliver studied agricultural engineering, became a coffee taster, and today dedicates himself entirely to producing unique coffees and researching machinery that makes life easier for producers.

Valle del Cauca & The Sugarcane EA Process

From a cooperative nestled between the National Park of Tatamá and the UNESCO-listed Serrania de los Paraguas at 1,700–2,100 masl. Sugarcane EA decaffeination uses a natural compound derived from Colombian sugarcane — not industrial chemicals. Low-pressure steam opens the bean; an ethyl acetate solution selectively removes up to 97% of caffeine without stripping flavor.
